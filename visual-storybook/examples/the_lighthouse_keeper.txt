THE LIGHTHOUSE KEEPER

BEAT 1 — THE CALM

MAREN, a lighthouse keeper in her sixties, tends the great lamp on Skerry
Hollow, a basalt islet far off a cold northern coast. She has kept the light
alone for thirty years. Every evening she climbs the spiral stair, trims the
wick, winds the clockwork, and speaks to the lamp as if it were an old friend.

MAREN: Hold fast, old friend. One more night.

BEAT 2 — THE SIGNAL

At midnight Maren notices something impossible: a second light, far out at
sea, blinking back at her in the old keepers' code. Dash, dash, dot. "Keeper
to keeper." No ship is charted there. No lighthouse stands there. She answers
with the shutter lamp, hands trembling.

MAREN: Who's out there? That station went dark before I was born.

BEAT 3 — THE CROSSING

At dawn Maren rows out through the fog toward where the light had been. She
finds only a ring of calm water, a wooden chair bolted to a drowned stone
platform, and a brass shutter lamp, still warm. Engraved on its base is her
own name, and a date fifty years from now.

BEAT 4 — THE ANSWER

Maren rows home with the lamp. That night she sets it beside the great lens
and lights it. Far out at sea, right on schedule, the impossible light blinks
first. This time, Maren smiles and answers without fear.

MAREN: Keeper to keeper. I'll hold the light until you're home.
